=== RAID capacity and probability ===
== Usable capacity of 6 disks of 4 TB (raw 24 TB) ==
RAID 0  : 24 TB (100%)   survives 0 failures   write cost 1 I/O   read speed ~6x
RAID 1  : 12 TB (50% as 3 mirrored pairs; one 6-way mirror would give only 4 TB = 17%)
          pairs: survives 1 failure (2 only if in different pairs), write cost 2 I/Os
RAID 5  : 20 TB (83%)   survives 1 failure   write cost 4 I/Os (read old data+parity, write new data+parity)
RAID 6  : 16 TB (67%)   survives 2 failures  write cost 6 I/Os (two parities, extra parity calculation)
RAID 10 : 12 TB (50%)    survives 1 failure guaranteed, up to 3 (one per mirror pair); write cost 2 I/Os

== Probability of data loss after one disk has failed ==
assumptions: annual failure rate 3.0%, rebuild time 24 h -> p(a given disk fails during rebuild) = 0.00834%
RAID 1 (mirror of the failed disk must survive)   : 0.00834%
RAID 10 (same as RAID 1: only the partner matters) : 0.00834%
RAID 5 (any of the 5 other disks failing)         : 0.04172%
RAID 6 (two more of the 5 remaining must fail)    : 0.00000696%

Unrecoverable read error during rebuild (1 error per 1e14 bits), RAID 5 must read 20 TB = 1.60e+14 bits:
P(at least one URE) = 1-(1-1e-14)^bits = 79.8%  -> large RAID 5 arrays of big disks are risky; RAID 6/10 are safer
RAID 1/10 rebuild reads only 4 TB: P(URE) = 27.4%
